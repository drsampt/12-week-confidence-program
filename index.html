WK 1
First impressions
Power Posture & The Pause
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Pause Entry
Walk to a mirror or doorway. Enter the space and pause for 2 full seconds before moving — scan the room slowly, then step forward. Repeat 10 times. You're rewiring the rush instinct that makes you look anxious.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Handshake Mastery
Practice the firm handshake drill: web-to-web contact, 2–3 pumps, eye contact held. Say "Great to meet you" out loud every rep. 10 rounds. The goal is automatic — no thinking when it counts.
✓
Day 3
5 min
The Slow Smile
Hold a neutral face in the mirror for 2 seconds, then let a genuine smile spread from the eyes out — never flash or snap it on. Practice 10 times. This is your "I see you" move. It reads as confident, not eager.
✓
Day 4
5 min
The Name Anchor
Write down 5 names. "Meet" each one: extend your hand, make eye contact, say "Great to meet you, [Name]." The name echo makes people feel genuinely seen. Practice until it's reflex, not effort.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Posture Reset Protocol
Set 5 phone alerts throughout today. At each alert: chest up, shoulders back, chin level, jaw relaxed. Hold 60 seconds. This isn't a one-day fix — it's building a new default. The body teaches the mind.
WK 2
Own the room without words
Eye Contact & Space
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Triangle Technique
Practice on your reflection: rotate eye contact between left eye, right eye, and mouth in a slow triangle. Aim for natural, not robotic. This is how confident people hold attention without staring people down.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Space Claiming
Practice standing and sitting without shrinking. Feet shoulder-width, arms relaxed at sides — not crossed, not tucked. Hold each position for 90 seconds. Claiming space is a social signal, not arrogance.
✓
Day 3
5 min
The Deliberate Walk
Walk to 5 different spots in your home as if entering a networking event each time. Purposeful pace, head level, eyes forward, shoulders back. Not rushed, not dragging. Walk like you've been here before.
✓
Day 4
5 min
The Attentive Nod
Record yourself for 2 minutes responding to an imagined conversation. Watch it back: are you nodding, tilting your head slightly, showing micro-expressions? Active listening is visible — practice making yours show.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Confident Rest Face
5 minutes of mirror work finding your "at ease" expression: relaxed jaw, soft eyes, slight upward corners of the mouth. This is your default public face. Most people walk around looking worried. You won't.
WK 3
Make your voice work for you
Voice Control & Presence
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
Belly Breathing
One hand on chest, one on belly. Breathe so only the belly hand moves. 5 full minutes. Diaphragmatic breathing deepens your voice naturally, lowers your heart rate under pressure, and eliminates that tight, high-pitched stress voice.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Pace Finder
Read any paragraph out loud at 3 speeds: rushing (too fast, sounds nervous), slow (too deliberate, sounds unsure), then your "I'm telling you something important" pace. Record all three. Your confident pace is the deliverable.
✓
Day 3
5 min
Kill the Filler
Record yourself talking for 3 minutes on any topic. Count every "um," "uh," and "like." Now replay — replace each one with a 1-second pause. The pause sounds more confident than the filler every time. This is your highest-ROI vocal upgrade.
✓
Day 4
5 min
Room Projection
Practice the same sentence at 3 volumes: intimate conversation, conference table, speaking across a large room. Projection comes from breath support — not from straining or shouting. Find where your voice carries without effort.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Tone Warmth
Say "Tell me more about that" three ways: flat (robotic), cold (dismissive), then warm and genuinely curious. Practice the warm version 15 times until it's your automatic response. Warmth in your tone signals safety — people open up to it.

WK 4
Never run out of things to say
Conversation Architecture
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The FORD Drill
Write 2 genuine questions each for Family, Occupation, Recreation, and Dreams. Say each out loud naturally. This is your emergency conversation toolkit — not a script, but a menu. When you go blank, you reach for it.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Observation Openers
Write 10 genuine, situational conversation starters based on something you'd actually observe — their work, a detail in the room, something you heard them mention. Not compliments — observations. These are far more memorable and far less creepy.
✓
Day 3
5 min
One Layer Down
Practice follow-up questions that dig one level deeper instead of changing subjects. "Where are you from?" → "What's the biggest difference living here vs. there?" Go deeper, not broader. This is how surface talk becomes real conversation.
✓
Day 4
5 min
The Callback
Write 5 short conversation scenarios. In each one, practice referencing something mentioned earlier: "Going back to what you said about X…" Callbacks show you actually listened. In a world of half-listeners, this makes you unforgettable.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Exit Gracefully
Write and practice 5 polished conversation exits. Example: "It's been great talking — I want to catch a few people before they leave." Smooth, warm, not abrupt. Ending well is as important as starting well — it's the last thing they remember.
WK 5
Be the most memorable person there
Storytelling & Deep Listening
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Story Spine
Pick one real story from your life. Structure it in exactly this shape: Context (10 sec) → Conflict (20 sec) → Resolution (15 sec) → Lesson (5 sec). Time it. Practice out loud. A structured story is 3x more memorable than a ramble.
✓
Day 2
5 min
The Sensory Detail
Take 3 of your stories. Add one vivid sensory detail to each — not "a busy restaurant" but "there were 30 people and someone at the next table was laughing way too loud." Specific details are what make stories feel real. Practice all three out loud.
✓
Day 3
5 min
Emotion First
Reopen 5 different stories by leading with the emotion before the event: "I was genuinely embarrassed when…" or "The strangest calm came over me right before…" People lean in for emotion. They stay for the story. Practice each out loud.
✓
Day 4
5 min
The Echo Technique
Play any podcast, video, or voice memo. Practice repeating the last 3 words of each sentence as a question. "…and then I quit." → "You just quit?" This signals deep listening and prompts people to expand. Drill it until it's natural, not mechanical.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Punch Line First
Take your 3 best stories. Lead with the most interesting part first, then fill in the setup: "I ended up on stage at a conference where I knew no one — here's how that happened." Leads that make people want the next sentence. Practice all three.
WK 6
Command group settings
Group Dynamics
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The 40/60 Balance
In your next real conversation, consciously aim to speak 40% and listen 60%. Afterward, journal one specific thing you learned about the other person that you wouldn't have if you'd talked more. Curiosity is a charisma superpower.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Open Stance Drill
Practice three standing positions: closed (arms crossed, feet together), neutral (hands in pockets, feet slightly apart), open (arms relaxed, feet shoulder-width, body angled toward others). Feel the difference. Your body broadcasts your intent before you speak.
✓
Day 3
5 min
Inclusive Eye Contact
Imagine a group of 4 people around you. Practice making brief, warm eye contact with each person in turn — naturally rotating, not scanning robotically. The goal: everyone in the group feels acknowledged. This is how you command a room without dominating it.
✓
Day 4
5 min
Energy Calibration
Think of 3 social environments: quiet dinner, cocktail party, sports event. Practice physically and vocally calibrating your energy to each one, then elevating it slightly above the room's baseline. You match, then you lead. That's social influence.
✓
Day 5
5 min
The Group Entry
Write and practice 3 ways to join an existing conversation circle: the physical approach angle, your opening line, and how you establish yourself within 60 seconds without hijacking the conversation. Practice each scenario out loud from start to finish.

WK 7
Make successful people want to talk to you
Networking with High-Achievers
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
Value-First Mindset
Write 3 genuine ways you could add value to someone you admire — before asking for anything. An article, an intro, a resource, a specific compliment about their work. This is the operating system that separates memorable networkers from forgettable ones.
✓
Day 2
5 min
The Research Opener
Find something public about 3 people you'd like to meet — a talk, article, decision, or achievement. Draft a genuine, curious opening line for each one. Not flattery — real curiosity about something specific. This is how you get 5 minutes with anyone.
✓
Day 3
5 min
The Memorable Intro
Write your 15-second personal introduction: who you are, what you do unusually well, and a hook that makes them want to ask more. No job titles — just outcomes. "I help athletes stay in their sport through injury" beats "I'm a physical therapist" every time. Drill it out loud.
✓
Day 4
5 min
The 24-Hour Follow-Up
Write a follow-up template: reference one specific thing from the conversation, add one line of value (an idea, resource, or thought), keep it 3 sentences max. This is how you get remembered in a world where nobody follows up. Practice writing 3 drafts for hypothetical scenarios.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Common Ground Fast
In 2–3 minutes of conversation, find one genuine shared interest: life stage, goals, places, frustrations, ambitions. Practice asking questions that uncover overlap. Write 8 questions designed to surface common ground quickly. Shared ground is the fastest path to real connection.
WK 8
Turn on your likeability at will
Natural Charm & Wit
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Specific Compliment
Write 10 genuine, specific compliments — not "you seem smart" but "the way you framed that tradeoff was something I hadn't considered." Specific compliments hit differently because they prove you actually paid attention. Say all 10 out loud until they feel natural, not scripted.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Playful Observation
Practice making one light, warm observation that shows you're paying attention: "You clearly know your stuff here — how long have you been obsessed with this?" Curiosity + warmth = charm. Write 5 playful observations for different contexts, then say them out loud until the tone is right.
✓
Day 3
5 min
The Unexpected Answer
Take 5 common small-talk questions ("What do you do?" / "How's it going?" / "What brings you here?") and write one genuine, unexpected answer for each. Not clever for clever's sake — genuinely real. Unexpected answers make you interesting. Predictable answers make you forgettable.
✓
Day 4
5 min
Self-Aware Humor
Identify one quirk, contradiction, or irony about yourself you can reference lightly — something that humanizes you without undermining you. Practice one self-aware comment per conversation, max. Practiced self-awareness reads as confidence. Excessive self-deprecation reads as insecurity.
✓
Day 5
5 min
Genuine Laughter
Notice the difference between performed laughter and real laughter in your daily interactions today. Practice the 2-second reaction window: let things land before you respond. Real humor is received, not performed. When something is actually funny, let your body show it — don't manage it.
WK 9
Replace anxiety with preparation
Social Anxiety Management
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
Box Breathing
Breathe in 4 counts, hold 4, breathe out 4, hold 4. Repeat 5 rounds — that's 80 seconds. Then practice this before one stressful social interaction today. Box breathing directly activates the parasympathetic nervous system. Your nervous system is trainable. Start now.
✓
Day 2
5 min
The Reframe Drill
Write 5 anxious thoughts you commonly have in social situations. Rewrite each as a neutral, evidence-based observation. "They think I'm boring" → "I don't have data on what they think. What I can control is how I engage." Anxiety lives in the story, not the situation.
✓
Day 3
5 min
Best / Worst / Likely
For an upcoming social event you're anxious about, map three scenarios: worst realistic case, best realistic case, most likely case. Write all three out. You'll find the most likely case is much closer to fine than your anxiety predicts. Do this every time before a high-stakes event.
✓
Day 4
5 min
The 5-Minute Rule
Before any social situation you're dreading, commit to only 5 minutes. Write your exit line in advance. Having a planned exit reduces the threat response that makes you avoid the event entirely. Knowing you can leave is often what lets you stay. Practice the script out loud.
✓
Day 5
5 min
The Anchor Pose
Choose one power posture: chest open, shoulders back, chin level, weight evenly distributed. Hold it for 2 full minutes in private. Practice this before every social situation this week. Research from Harvard, Stanford, and Oxford all show this shifts your internal state before you've said a word.

WK 10

Professional Presence
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Elevator Pitch
Write and practice a 30-second introduction built for professional events. Lead with outcome, not title: not "I'm a PT" but "I help competitive athletes stay in their sport through injury." Time it. Say it 10 times until it sounds like belief, not a rehearsed line.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Name + Value
Reduce your intro to its most essential form: name + one clear, immediate value statement. This is your opening sentence in every new professional interaction. Drill it until it feels natural, not rehearsed. Simplicity is confidence. Complexity is insecurity.
✓
Day 3
5 min
The Confident Ask
Today, make one direct request — professional or personal. No hedging ("I was wondering if maybe you could…"). State the ask cleanly. Confident people ask for things. Write 3 asks you've been putting off and practice saying each one out loud with direct, clear language.
✓
Day 4
5 min
Early Voice
In your next meeting, group setting, or social gathering, speak up within the first 5 minutes. Even one sentence. Early participation signals engagement and belonging. Silence in the first 10 minutes makes it progressively harder to speak. Practice your "first 5 minutes" line for 3 upcoming scenarios.
✓
Day 5
5 min
The Original Insight
Practice sharing one genuine perspective per conversation — not what you think they want to hear, but what you actually think. Write 5 opinions you hold about your field, your work, or your life that you've never said out loud. Pick the best one. Say it. Conviction is magnetic.
WK 11
Be the person they think of first
Leadership Presence
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Credit Share
Practice publicly acknowledging a collaborator while noting your own contribution: "They built the foundation — I made sure it actually got used." Write 3 examples from your own work. People who share credit generously are trusted. People who hoard it are resented.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Decisive Language
Record yourself for 3 minutes. Audit every hedge: "I think maybe," "sort of," "I guess," "kind of." Replace each with a direct statement — or silence. Vague language signals low status. Decisive language signals leadership. Write your 5 most common hedges and their direct replacements.
✓
Day 3
5 min
Vision in Three Sentences
Write your entrepreneurial vision in exactly 3 sentences: what you're building, who it's for, and why it matters right now. Practice saying it until it sounds like belief, not a pitch deck. Vision spoken with conviction attracts people who want to help build it.
✓
Day 4
5 min
Proactive Reach-Out
Contact one person in your network today with pure value — an article, a congratulation on something specific, an intro, a thought that made you think of them. No ask attached. Write the message in 5 minutes and send it. Relationships maintained are opportunities not lost.
✓
Day 5
5 min
The Expertise Signal
In your next conversation, share one specific, useful insight from your field — not "I know about this" but the actual insight itself. Practice with 3 real insights from your work that most people don't know. Expertise shown is more powerful than expertise claimed.
WK 12
This is who you are now
Integration & Identity
▾
✓
Day 1
5 min
The Highlight Reel
From the last 11 weeks, identify your 3 strongest skills — the exercises that genuinely changed how you show up. Write one sentence on each. Then read all three out loud. This is your new evidence base. You're not trying to become someone else. You became more yourself.
✓
Day 2
5 min
Stress Test
Imagine your 3 most challenging social scenarios — the rooms where you still feel most out of your depth. Write out exactly how the version of you from Week 12 handles each one. Not how you wish you'd act. How you would actually act now. This is the difference.
✓
Day 3
5 min
Brand Story Final
Record your personal brand story as an entrepreneur — exactly 60 seconds: who you are, what you're building, why someone should remember you. Watch it back. This is not a pitch. It's your identity spoken out loud. If it sounds like you, you're done. If it doesn't, record it again.
✓
Day 4
5 min
Network Activation
Reach out to 2–3 meaningful contacts with a genuine, personal message. Reference something real — something you remember about them or have been thinking about. No pitch. Pure relationship. You've spent 12 weeks building who you are. Now let the people who matter see it.
✓
Day 5
5 min
The Capstone
Set a timer for 5 minutes. Record yourself as if you just arrived at a professional event. Walk through your first 5 minutes: the entry pause, the first approach, the introduction, the question, the listen, the callback, the exit. Watch it back. Compare it to where you started. This is your new baseline.
